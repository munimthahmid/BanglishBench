## Current Thesis Claim

Competent Qwen models can answer some Bangla and English curriculum-style
questions, but their performance drops sharply when the same Bangla content is
written in Latin-script Banglish. Across several compact open models, Banglish
is also much weaker than English despite using Latin characters. The Qwen drop
persists even when Banglish is token-cheaper than native Bangla. Simple prompts
do not solve it, and self-normalization helps one model while hurting another,
showing that Banglish mitigation is a real model-dependent research problem
rather than a prompt wording issue.

The frozen-v5 all-200 slice strengthens the core claim. Qwen3-4B and
Qwen2.5-7B 8-bit retain negative Banglish-vs-Bangla confidence intervals.
Qwen2.5-3B retains a negative point estimate but its all-200 interval reaches
zero; the strict-197 sensitivity remains negative.

Gemini 3.5 Flash changes the shape of the evidence rather than erasing it.
Its strict frozen-v5 all-200 row is much stronger in absolute accuracy
(163/200 Bangla, 136/200 reviewed Banglish, 144/200 English), but reviewed
Banglish is still -13.5 points behind Bangla. On BEnQA the gap is smaller but
present (133/144, 124/144, 129/144). On BanglaMATH, strict scoring shows a
large Banglish drop (30/56, 12/56, 15/56), while numeric/unit sensitivity
nearly closes it (36/56, 34/56, 35/56). The resulting claim is that frontier
models reduce semantic Banglish failure but do not remove code-mixed
answer-format and normalization risk.

GPT-5.5 low on the full validation-200 v5 triad changes the frontier boundary
again. Strict reviewed-Banglish accuracy is 169/200, only -1.5 points behind
Bangla, and secondary sensitivity is 174/200, +0.5 points ahead of Bangla. The
result should be used as population evidence that top-tier reasoning can nearly
remove the Banglish gap, while the cap-256 probe and 53,632 logged reasoning
tokens show that output-cap and hidden-reasoning budget are part of the
protocol.

The BnSentMix external layer adds a separate natural code-mixed sentiment task:
Qwen2.5-3B scores 89/200, Qwen2.5-7B 8-bit scores 98/200, and Qwen3-4B scores
99/200 with 600/600 valid labels. Qwen2.5 scaling nearly matches Qwen3 on
headline accuracy, but 7B overpredicts neutral while Qwen3 overpredicts
positive. The complementarity audit shows the best single model is 99/200,
while an any-model diagnostic oracle reaches 154/200; use this as error-overlap
evidence, not deployable accuracy. The routing dev-test audit gives a weak
candidate, majority vote with Qwen2.5-7B fallback, at 106/200 under hash5 CV,
but ordered block40 CV drops to 84/200. Use BnSentMix for ecological validity
and complementarity stress testing, not as a paired script-gap estimate or
locked mitigation.

Model-family nuance:

- Phi-3.5-mini on validation-200 v4 scores Bangla 38/200, Banglish 40/200,
  English 80/200.
- Qwen3-1.7B no-thinking on validation-200 v4 scores Bangla 34/200, Banglish
  36/200, English 61/200.
- Their Banglish-vs-Bangla intervals cross zero, while Banglish-vs-English is
  clearly negative for both models.
- Use these runs as breadth and nuance, not as the anchor for the
  Banglish-below-Bangla claim.
